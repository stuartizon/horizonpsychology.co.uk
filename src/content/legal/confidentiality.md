## When might confidentiality be limited?

There are some circumstances in which information may need to be shared without your consent. These include situations where:

- There is a serious concern about your safety or wellbeing, or the safety of another person.
- There are safeguarding concerns involving a child or an adult at risk of abuse or neglect.
- Information must be disclosed to meet a legal obligation, such as a court order, or where disclosure is otherwise justified by law or a relevant public interest.

Wherever possible and appropriate, these concerns will be discussed with you before information is shared. However, there may be circumstances where it is necessary to act without informing you beforehand, for example where doing so could increase risk or cause an unsafe delay.

Where information needs to be shared, this will be limited to what is necessary and proportionate to the circumstances.

## Clinical supervision

Regular clinical supervision is an important part of maintaining safe, reflective and effective psychological practice. Emma discusses aspects of her clinical work in supervision to support professional reflection, clinical decision-making and the quality of care provided.

Where possible, information is anonymised and identifying details are minimised. Supervisors are also expected to respect confidentiality. In some circumstances, limited identifying information may need to be discussed where necessary to support safe and effective practice.

## Confidentiality in supervision

Confidentiality is an important part of both clinical and research supervision. Information shared during supervision is treated with care and discretion and will not ordinarily be shared outside the supervisory relationship without consent, unless there is a legal or ethical reason to do so.

Clinical supervision may involve discussions about cases, professional practice and clinical decision-making. Supervisees are expected to anonymise client information wherever possible and maintain confidentiality in accordance with their own professional and organisational responsibilities.

Research supervision may involve discussions about research ideas, projects, findings and professional development. Information relating to research participants must be handled in accordance with relevant ethical requirements, data protection obligations and research governance procedures.

As with therapy, confidentiality may need to be limited where there are serious concerns about safety, safeguarding issues or legal obligations. Wherever possible and appropriate, such concerns will be discussed with the relevant person before information is shared.

## Confidentiality and your records

Clinical records are maintained securely and handled in accordance with applicable data protection legislation and relevant professional standards.
