# Design snapshot, 2026-10-01

A copy of the site's design from Claude Design (claude.ai/design), kept here so anyone working on an issue can read the styles and copy it intended ([ADR 0017](../../adr/0017-keep-a-snapshot-of-the-design-in-the-repo.md)). It's a reference, not a spec: where it disagrees with the code, see [Which source wins](#which-source-wins).

It's temporary. Once the redesign (#14) is built, this folder is deleted in the clean-up (#13), and the code is the record of the design.

## What's here

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `website.dc.html` | The website design: every page's markup, inline styles and copy. Pages are `<sc-if value="{{ isHome }}">` blocks, and components come in as `<x-import component-from-global-scope="…Name">`. |
| `design-system/_ds_bundle.js` | The design system's components, compiled from JSX. Search for `function Name(` to read one, for example `function Quote(`. Its `ui_kits/website` pages are an earlier version of the site. |
| `design-system/tokens/*.css` | The design system's tokens: palette, type, spacing, shape, motion and base element styles. |

These files are for reading. They don't render on their own: the export's runtime, images and fonts aren't included.

Left out of the export: the design system's readme, whose rules for writing copy are in [CONTRIBUTING.md](../../../CONTRIBUTING.md#writing-copy) and whose other notes are covered by the tokens or out of date; the compiled `Horizon Psychology website.html` (9 MB, the same design bundled with its runtime), the prototype images, and the uploaded brief, page copy and wireframes, which are working documents rather than design.

## Which source wins

The design system was settled first, the website design was iterated on top of it, and more decisions have been made since in issues, pull requests and code. When they disagree, the later one wins:

1. **The code on `main`, the [ADRs](../../adr/README.md) and the issues.**
2. **The website design** (`website.dc.html`).
3. **The design system** (`design-system/`).

### Known differences

Where the site departs from the design on purpose. A pull request that departs from the design adds a line here, so nobody "fixes" it back.

- **Tokens.** `global.css` keeps the palette and shared scales, without the design's role aliases such as `--surface-sage` or `--color-text-muted`. Components use palette colours directly ([ADR 0012](../../adr/0012-small-global-token-set-and-component-owned-styles.md)). The mapping: `surface-page` is `background`, `surface-card` is `panel`, `surface-quiet` is `cream`, `surface-sage` is `sage-50`, `text-body` is `ink`, `text-muted` is `ink-soft`, `text-heading` is `sage-800`, `eyebrow` is `sage-600`.
- **Amber, not sand.** Sand was the old warm colour; the design moved to amber. The design system's tokens keep `--color-sand-*` only as aliases for amber, and some of its components still use those names. The site has amber only.
- **Breakpoints.** The design changes layout at five widths. The site uses two, tablet at 48rem and desktop at 64rem ([ADR 0014](../../adr/0014-two-site-wide-breakpoints-in-rem.md)).
- **Section spacing.** The space between sections is `clamp(56px, 7vw, 96px)`, about 1.5 times the space below the nav, rather than the design's `clamp(72px, 10vw, 128px)`, twice that space. The top of the footer has the same space as below the nav, `clamp(36px, 5vw, 72px)`, rather than a 64px maximum (#100).
- **Fonts** are self-hosted with Fontsource, not loaded from Google Fonts ([ADR 0004](../../adr/0004-self-host-fonts-with-fontsource.md)).
- **Icons** are imported from the `@phosphor-icons/core` package ([ADR 0016](../../adr/0016-phosphor-icons-from-the-npm-package.md)).
- **Lead text.** The home page intro, each service's summary, the FAQs, Research and Contact page intros are normal body text, not the design's larger, softer lead style (#5, #6, #8, #9, #11).
- **Home page.** Testimonials move to the About page (#5, #7). The hero and "What we offer" go into two columns at the tablet breakpoint, rather than when the design's auto-fitting grid has room, and the service cards are stacked on a phone, 2×2 from tablet width and four in a row from desktop width, where titles wrap until about 1180px (#77). The hero's portrait lines up with the top of the heading's capitals, and its intro is the heading's subtitle. "What we offer" is also normal body text, its first line's capitals level with its heading's, and from desktop width it splits in half like the hero, so their columns line up (#246).
- **Service card.** Fixed 24px padding rather than `clamp(24px, 2.4vw, 32px)`, so titles fit on one line on wide screens; and its "Find out more →" is its own small semibold link, not the link button (#67). It shows the session length but not the fee, which is on the service's page (#135).
- **Intro call panel** (the design's "Start with a free 15-minute call" panel). The heading has its own row, and the button sits beside the paragraph from tablet up, or centred below it on a phone, rather than centred across the heading and paragraph and wrapping at about 700px (#68).
- **FAQ answers** line up with the question text, running up to the toggle's column, rather than stopping at the prose measure (#73).
- **Service page intro.** The fees panel sits beside the description from tablet width up, rather than dropping below it until the description and panel both fit at their preferred widths. On narrow phones the title shrinks so it stays on one line beside its icon, and the icon is sized to the title (1.05em) rather than a fixed 34px. The questions also sit beside their heading from tablet width up, with the heading column narrowing first (#75). The fees panel's top is level with the top of the title's capitals (#246).
- **Service page photos.** Each photo is shown at its own proportions, uncropped, rather than at 16:7, so nothing important is cut off (#75).
- **FAQs page.** Every FAQ is on the page, in five groups of four: clinical and research supervision are separate groups, rather than the design's single group of four supervision questions. The groups have no eyebrows, and from tablet width up they run on as one list with a single divider between them, rather than being spaced apart. The heading and intro run the full width, not the prose measure. Wherever questions sit beside their heading, here and on the service pages, the first line of the heading column, the eyebrow if there is one, is level with the first question rather than with the top of the list (#8). The first two groups are "Before you begin therapy" and "How therapy works" rather than "Before you begin" and "How the work runs", and the intro ends with a link to the Contact page (#281). Ten of the answers are Emma's rewritten copy, and four of them link to the Fees and cancellations, Confidentiality and Privacy policy pages. The two couples questions under "How therapy works" begin "In couples therapy," so they read clearly outside the Couples therapy page (#282).
- **About page.** The portrait is the square photo of Emma at her desk, shown uncropped rather than at 4:5. The HCPC and BABCP logos under the bio are the bodies' official artwork, with the white margin trimmed from the BABCP logo so the two look the same size. They're centred under the portrait rather than on the left under the bio (#297), and don't fade on hover. The HCPC logo links to Emma's entry on the register rather than its search page (#92). The "Get in touch" and "See publications" buttons are centred under both of the approach columns, rather than in a section further down under the text column. Below tablet width, the intro and approach text run the full width rather than stopping at the prose measure (#7). On a tablet, the introduction wraps around the portrait, and on a phone the portrait comes after the second paragraph to break up the text (#176). A photo of the bookshelf, where the design has no image, sits beside "How Emma works": in its own column on a desktop, with the text wrapping around it on a tablet, and between the two paragraphs on a phone (#175). From tablet width, the portrait and the bookshelf photo line up with the top of their heading's capitals, and from desktop width the approach splits in half like the introduction, so its text starts under the portrait (#246).
- **Testimonials carousel.** Testimonials slide in from the right (or the left, going back) and the last one slides out, rather than fading with a 32px nudge. The quotes are smaller than the site's quote style, `clamp(1rem, 0.75rem + 1.1vw, 1.3rem)`, as some are long. The arrows are `sage-600` rather than `sage-300`, and the buttons for the testimonials not showing are a 30/70 mix of `sage-600` and `sage-300`, a little darker than the design's, against the current one's `sage-800`. Phones have no arrows, so the quote runs the full width, and visitors use the buttons or swipe. A shorter quote sits halfway down the space the longest needs, rather than at the top. The buttons are plain buttons marked `aria-current`, not tabs, and changes are only announced to screen readers once a visitor moves between testimonials, not while it moves on by itself (#7).
- **Research page.** The photo of Emma's thesis sits after the intro paragraph, where the design has no image. Every publication's title links to its DOI, and those links and the ResearchGate and Academia.edu links open in a new tab, with a small arrow (Phosphor's `arrow-up-right`) after each to show it. There's no line above the first publication or below the last, and the capitals of the first one's authors are level with the heading's (#246). From tablet width up, the introduction takes a third of the width, or more if the button needs it, rather than wrapping onto its own row. On a phone the enquiry button is centred, and the introduction's lines fill the width rather than being evened out (#9). Below the publications, under an eyebrow-styled "Acknowledged in" heading, a second list, which the design doesn't have, gives the papers that acknowledge Dr Izon without her being an author (#106).
- **Contact page.** The required-field asterisks are `amber-800` rather than `amber-600`, which is too pale against white to read. The phone layout and the help notice follow the design, but the form sits beside the introduction from the tablet breakpoint rather than at 800px, with its top level with the top of the heading's capitals (#246). There's no email panel: the introduction links to the email address and says sessions are online or face-to-face, and the help notice is headed “If you need urgent support” rather than “If you need help now” (#181). The “Send enquiry” and “Send another enquiry” buttons are centred in the form rather than on its left (#148). A failed send shows a message with the email address (#11). The privacy policy link by the consent checkbox opens in a new tab, so a visitor doesn't lose what they've typed. Only screen readers are told; it has no arrow, which marks links to other sites (#161). While an enquiry sends, which the design doesn't cover, the button keeps its colour and width, and its label gives way to a spinner and “Sending…”. The spinner is still for people who prefer reduced motion (#147).
- **Button** takes a `variant` prop (primary, secondary or link) where the design's takes `type`, so `type` can be the native button type. A form button can take a `busyLabel`, shown with a spinner while a script marks it busy (#147).
- **Panels.** The white panels (the intro call panel and the enquiry form), the sage fees panel and the amber help notice share one `Panel` component, with the panel radius and padding for all three. The help notice is the design's `Callout`, which has the smaller card radius and padding (#122).
- **Switch.** Only the hint is shown, beside the switch, and it changes with it; the label names the switch for screen readers but isn't shown, and there's no On/Off word. Off has a `line-strong` border and an `ink-soft` thumb, which gives it 3:1 contrast, and its border darkens to `ink` on hover, with no halo, as the site's other controls have none. Disabled is off in paler greys, a `line` border and a `line-strong` thumb, rather than a dashed border (#240).
- **Section heading** takes a `level` prop where the design's takes `as` (#65). It has a one-line `subtitle` in the lead style, used for Emma's credentials on the About page, rather than the design's `lead` paragraph (#7).
- **Legal pages.** The text runs the full width of the page, rather than stopping at the feature measure, so the margins either side are even. The lead is normal body text, as on the other pages, rather than the lead style. There's no shared closing note: each page ends with Emma's own line on where to send questions. Registration bodies are named in full the first time, with their acronyms (#124). On the fees and cancellations page, the fees are a list of the services, each linking to its page, with its fee from `services.ts`, rather than a sentence listing them (#126), under a "Session fees" heading. The page is at `/fees-and-cancellations/` rather than `/terms-and-conditions/` (#185).
- **Footer.** The link groups are headed "About Horizon" and "Information" rather than "Practice" and "Practical", and there's no line on registration and emergencies below them (#182, #183). The emergency signposting is on the Contact page. A copyright line, "© 2026 Horizon Psychology" with the current year, sits below the groups (#184). On a phone the groups have no eyebrows and run together as one list of links, in two columns once two 9rem columns fit and in one below that, rather than three stacked, headed groups (#294).
- **404 page.** The design has none. It's laid out like the home page's hero, with the photo of an hourglass beside the heading from tablet width up (#153).
- **Sending the contact form without JavaScript.** The browser lands on `/contact/sent/` or `/contact/not-sent/`: copies of the Contact page with the result in place of the form, kept out of search results (#145).
- **Not built:** the blog page and its link in the footer, which are out of scope for launch (#14).

## Refreshing the snapshot

When the design changes in Claude Design:

1. Export the project as a handoff bundle for Claude Code.
2. Copy into a new dated folder, `docs/design/YYYY-MM-DD/`:
   - `project/Horizon Psychology website.dc.html` as `website.dc.html`
   - from `project/_ds/<design system>/`: `_ds_bundle.js`, `styles.css` and `tokens/`
3. Check the files for anything that shouldn't be public, such as client details or keys.
4. Copy this README across, update its date, and review the known differences against the new design.
5. Delete the old folder, and update the links to it in CONTRIBUTING.md and the open issues.
